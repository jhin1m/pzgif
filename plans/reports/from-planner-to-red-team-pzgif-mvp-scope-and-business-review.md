# Red-team review — PZGIF MVP plan (product / business / legal / UX)

Reviewer stance: hostile. Target: `plans/260805-0001-pzgif-mvp-9-browser-native-gif-tools-discord-presets/`.
Ratified decisions (AGPL, 9 tools, `provider = none`, 1+4 Discord routes) are treated as fixed; their **consequences** are not.

Read: `plan.md`, `phase-01`–`phase-11`, `docs/tech-stack.md`, `docs/design-guidelines.md`, `docs/wireframe/{index,tool-compressor,tool-mp4-to-gif,discord-preset,states}.html`, both `research-260804-2343-*` reports, the five `research-260804-2230-*` reports, `from-bootstrap-to-planner-*`.

---

## 0. The one-line verdict

The plan is an excellent **engineering** plan and a **weak business plan wearing an engineering plan's clothes**. Every gate, budget and taxonomy points at "does the encoder work". Almost nothing points at "does this ever make money, and how would we know". Three of the four things that actually determine revenue — domain age, indexed page count, and measurement of tool usage — are either deferred to the last phase, under-scoped, or absent entirely.

## 1. CRITICAL

| # | Finding | Why it goes wrong | Concrete fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **The domain is bought in Phase 11.** `pzgif.com` purchase sits in the launch checklist (`phase-11`, step 8), ~5 weeks out. | The business is "long-tail SEO compounding over 12-18 months" (bootstrap handoff §SEO). The binding resource is *indexed age*, not code. Buying the domain at week 5 throws away 5 weeks of crawl history and delays every downstream date — AdSense application, Journey's 1k-sessions/30d clock, first dollar — by the same 5 weeks. | C2 | **iOS is structurally excluded from the flagship conversion tool, and nothing in the plan says so out loud.** | `phase-04` iOS tier: 30 MB frame budget, **hard cap 57 frames**, 480 px. At the wireframe's own defaults (15 fps, 480 px) that is **3.8 s of output GIF**. `tool-mp4-to-gif.html` promises "up to 150 MB · up to 60 seconds". Mobile is the majority of search traffic for consumer utility queries; iOS Safari is the majority of mobile web revenue traffic in US/UK — the exact geography the RPM model depends on. The plan treats this as a memory-engineering detail (Phase 4) and a copy-audit item (Phase 11). It is a **market-size** fact: the flagship tool works for a few seconds of video on the highest-value traffic segment. | (a) Make the limits caption **runtime-computed per device tier**, never static copy. (b) Add a **mobile default profile** (10 fps, 320 px → ~5.7 s at the same budget) so the refusal threshold is at least in "usable meme length" territory. (c) Add a Phase 1 gate — measure real iOS output duration at *acceptable quality*, and if it lands under ~5 s, decide explicitly whether `mp4-to-gif` ships mobile-first, desktop-first, or with a device-gated flow. (d) Put the number in the plan's Goals table so it survives. |
| C3 | **The plan produces 14 pages and no content engine — the AdSense "low value content" gate is the whole revenue path and the plan under-shoots the documented bar.** | Research (`research-260804-2230-ads-eligibility…` line 7, `research-…-2343-nextjs…` D17) says: do not apply before **10-20 indexed *content* pages**, About with real identity, legal pages. The plan delivers 14 tool/preset pages with ≥400-word explainers and *zero* non-tool content (no guides, no comparison pages, no blog). Tool pages with an embedded app are the classic shape reviewers reject; 400 words is the floor, not a cushion. Meanwhile the only alternative networks are Journey (needs 1k sessions/30d, and **Grow-on-Next.js is unverified** — `phase-10` open Q1) or nothing. If AdSense rejects twice, the plan has no third path and no revenue mechanism at all. | Add a **Phase 9b: 6-10 genuine non-tool content pages** written during the build (they need no engine): "GIF vs MP4 vs WebP for the web", "Every Discord asset size limit in one table", "Why your GIF is 6 MB", "gifski vs standard GIF encoders (with samples)", "How to make a Discord emoji that isn't blurry". These double as link bait, as the G6 proof surface (see H2), and as the AdSense evidence pack. Publish them on the domain from C1 *while* the engine is being built, so they are indexed before the tools ship. |
| C4 | **No measurement of tool usage, job outcomes, or refusal rate — the operator cannot learn anything from launch.** | The only instrumentation in the plan is `web-vitals` → GA4 (`phase-10`), Sentry, and one line in `phase-04` step 14 logging `{container, codec, path}`. There is **no event for job started / refused by admission control / failed / completed / downloaded, per tool, per device tier**. Compounding it: consent is **deny-by-default globally** (`phase-10`), so GA4 sees only opted-in users — a minority, and a biased one. Consequence: after launch the operator cannot answer "which of the 9 tools do people use", "what % of mobile jobs are refused before decode", "do people who see the auto-fit result actually download". Those are precisely the questions that decide what to build in month 2, and whether the AGPL/gifski bet paid off. | Add a **consent-free, file-content-free counter path**: aggregate, cookieless, no identifiers (a `sendBeacon` to a Vercel edge route or a cookieless product-analytics tool) recording `{tool, deviceTier, outcome, durationBucket, inputSizeBucket}`. Legitimate-interest, no personal data, no consent gate — same footing as Sentry in the plan's own table. Add a Phase 10 success criterion: "a dashboard answers *which tools ran, and what fraction were refused* on day 1". |
| C5 | **Public AGPL repo also publishes the SEO copy — the only real moat — under a copyleft licence that lets competitors legally republish it.** | The plan puts hand-written per-tool prose in `src/content/*.tsx` (`phase-05`/`06`/`07`/`08`/`09`) inside a repo it makes public under AGPL-3.0 (`phase-02` step 8). AGPL is a *software* licence; shipping prose as `.tsx` in the same repo, with no separate notice, hands a competitor a defensible argument that the copy is licensed to them. The plan's own analysis says the moat is "SEO content + domain + traffic, not the tool code" (research 2343 §A1 option B) — and then open-sources the moat. A competitor with an aged domain can fork, rebrand, deploy, and outrank the original with the original's own words. This is ~1 hour of work and is the difference between "our code is free" and "our business is free". |

| # | Finding | Why it goes wrong | Concrete fix |

| H1 | **The plan never states the core hypothesis, so it cannot sequence toward testing it.** | `plan.md` has 8 Goals, all execution goals ("ship 9 tools", "CLS 0", "WCAG AA"). That is a 4-6 week pull-forward on every revenue milestone for roughly 3 days of work. | H4 | **"Offline-capable after first load" is a stated Goal with no implementing phase — and the FAQ promises it to users.** | `plan.md` Goal 2: "offline-capable after first load". No phase creates a service worker, a manifest, or any caching strategy. `tool-compressor.html` FAQ tells the user: *"Turning off your connection after the page loads also works."* On a reload with no network, the site is a browser error page. This is a promise the user is explicitly invited to test, and it fails. | Either (a) drop the goal and cut the sentence, or (b) add a minimal SW in Phase 2 caching the shell + `/wasm/*` (they are already `immutable`-cached; the SW is ~40 lines). (b) is cheap, is a genuine differentiator vs ezgif, and makes the strongest privacy demonstration in the copy actually true. Note the SW must not cache ad/analytics endpoints. |
| H5 | **Admission-control refusal is a hard dead end, and the whole product has no way to contact, retain, or follow up with anyone.** | `phase-04` error taxonomy routes `too-large-for-device` and `decode-failed` to "or Pro" — Pro does not exist. `phase-07` acknowledges the upsell target is missing and says the message "must stand on its own". There is **no email capture, no newsletter, no support address, no feedback widget, no contact page** anywhere in the plan or wireframes. A tool site with no retention mechanism and no contact surface is 100% dependent on re-acquiring every user through Google, forever. | (a) Refusal state offers a **one-click degraded run** ("Do it anyway at 320 px, 10 fps — 6 s of GIF") instead of a refusal, whenever any plan fits. The server binary can be kept separate (unmodified gifski via subprocess = the standard mere-aggregation position, per research 2343 §A1 option D); the **frontend cannot**, because the Pro UI is the same UI. Realistically: the entire frontend is AGPL forever. | Decide and write it down **now**, in `plan.md`: *"The PZGIF frontend is AGPL permanently. | H7 | **AGPL source-offer compliance is per-deployed-version, and the plan's mechanism (a footer link to a repo) does not guarantee it.** | GPLv3 §6, which AGPL incorporates, requires Corresponding Source **for the version conveyed**. It is a *ranking* page. A user who arrives from a SERP, uploads, and is told "not supported" pogo-sticks back to Google — the strongest negative engagement signal a new domain can generate, on a page whose whole purpose is to rank. The plan's own open Q3 asks whether to ship it and answers "ship it, but cut it first if Phase 7 runs long" — exactly backwards. | Three options, ranked: (1) build the ~150-line RIFF/ANMF splitter (`phase-07` calls it the correct long-term answer) — it is one afternoon and makes the page universally functional; (2) cut the page from MVP and ship it when (1) is done; (3) ship as-is. ## 6. What I could not fault

The engineering-integrity layer is genuinely unusual and should not be traded away under time pressure: admission control that refuses **before** decode rather than OOM-ing mid-job; a `ProgressBar` API shaped so a synthesised percentage is structurally impossible; E2E tests that assert on **decoded output bytes** rather than DOM state; the refusal to emit dead `FAQPage` markup or a fabricated `aggregateRating` for a cosmetic rich result; the per-preset Discord config with source URLs and verification dates, and the willingness to correct two "locked" documents rather than quietly build around them. The Phase-1-gates-before-product-code sequencing is the right shape, and the `provider = none` launch decision is correct on its own terms. The plan is also unusually honest about its own timeline slip and about which risks it has not retired.

## 7. Unresolved questions

1. **What is the actual iOS/mobile traffic share for these keywords, and what output duration is "useful"?** C2's severity is unknowable without it. One afternoon in a keyword tool or a competitor's public analytics answers it, and it may reorder the entire tool priority list.
2. **Does Journey/Grow work on Next.js?** Unverified since research; it is the only fallback if AdSense rejects. One email to Mediavine. Should be sent in week 1, not month 3.
3. **If G6 fails, does the project continue?** The plan has no answer. If gifski quality is not visible, the product is undifferentiated, the AGPL cost is wasted, and the honest options include "stop". That should be an explicit branch, decided before Phase 1 runs, not after.
4. **Who is the named operator on the About page, and are they willing to be publicly named?** Blocks AdSense (E-E-A-T), the Contact page, GDPR controller identification, and the DMCA agent if it is ever needed. Currently `phase-09` open Q1 and unowned.
5. **Is the content licence separable from the code licence in practice?** C5's fix assumes yes. It is the conservative reading (prose is not part of "the Program"), but if a future Pro tier embeds content into AGPL modules the boundary erodes. Decide the file layout now.
6. **What is the acceptable cut if week 4 arrives with Phases 6/7/8 unfinished?** The plan names Phases 6/7 as the cut line. This review argues the cut should be *polish and tool breadth*, not keyword surface — but that trade needs the operator's call, not a reviewer's.
7. **Is there a rollback plan for a Google manual action?** 14 near-identical pages on a zero-authority domain is the documented risk shape, the penalty is site-wide, and the plan's mitigation is "write good copy". If it happens anyway, what is the response? Currently unanswered.
